Why it matters

You already paid for this building. Here's what you own.

Most Cal State LA students commute. The U-SU is the part of campus that is useful whether or not you have class in the next hour — and it is already included in what you pay.

01

The four hours between your classes

A commuter's day has holes in it. This is where you sit them out: lounges, study rooms, nap pods, microwaves, outlets, and a chair that isn't the front seat of your car.

02

The things you'd otherwise pay for

The fitness center, the food pantry and everyday essentials are covered by the fee you already pay. No membership, no per-visit charge, no sign-up.

03

Nearly 100 student jobs

Between 90 and 100 students work here4 alongside 29 full-time staff — the largest student employer on campus, with schedules built around class times.

And if you are one of the students who never comes in — someone you know does. The pantry, the quiet floor and the cultural graduations are load-bearing for people who don't advertise that they need them.

What's inside the building

Events & Activities

Programming run by students, for students — from noon concerts to the five cultural graduation ceremonies.

Study & Rest Spaces

Quiet floors, group rooms, nap pods and somewhere to be between a 10 AM and a 2 PM class.

Play & Recreation

The fitness center, the Game Room and the GENE program — included, no membership.

Cross Cultural Centers

APISRC, CLSRC, GSRC and PASRC — staffed centers, open doors, and the Cultural Grads ceremonies.

Jobs & Leadership

U-Krew employment, the Board of Directors, and paid roles that fit around a class schedule.

Everyday Essentials

Food pantry, microwaves, lockers and the small things that make a long commuter day workable.

The part that doesn't add up on its own

If the 2007 fee had only kept pace with inflation, it would be $215.30 a semester today.5 The proposal is $227.25 — $11.95 more than inflation alone would explain. That difference is real, and it is there because catching up on inflation does not close a shortfall that is also driven by enrollment decline.3 Student fees currently cover 67% of what the U-SU costs to run; the sustainable range is 80–85%.2

Questions

Exactly how much, and when would it start?+

$90 more per semester. Your U-SU fee goes from $137.25 to $227.25 a semester — $454.50 a year instead of $274.50. Across a 16-week semester that is $5.63 a week. The effective term has not been set. [NEEDS FIGURE — effective term]

Does my financial aid cover it?+

That answer belongs to Cal State LA Financial Aid, not to the U-SU, and we are not going to approximate it. [NEEDS COPY — Financial Aid]

What is the 3% annual adjustment — is it a blank check?+

No. The proposed contract language allows the fee to rise by up to 3% a year for inflation. It is a ceiling, not a target, and it sits below the historical average rate of inflation — which is exactly why this page exists: the fee was set in 2007 and has not moved in nineteen years. The adjustment is there so that the U-SU never has to come back and ask for another $90 at once.

Why can't tuition or the university cover this?+

The U-SU is a separate 501(c)(3) nonprofit governed by a board chaired by an elected student. It is not part of the university's operating budget and tuition does not fund it. Student fees cover 67% of what it costs to run today; the sustainable range is 80–85%.

What happens if it doesn't pass?+

$2,000,000 has already been cut across FY 2024-25 and FY 2025-26 — 25% of the operating budget. On the current fee, the reserve falls from $8,364,353 in FY 2024-25 to $274,702 in FY 2029-30 and to −$2,065,518 in FY 2030-31. What gets reduced after that has not been decided. See the full section →

Who decided the amount?+

The U-SU Board of Directors, chaired by an elected student, working from the Fiscal Committee's April 10 2026 budget projection. [NEEDS COPY — board resolution and vote record]

I never use the U-SU — why am I paying?+

Fair question, and the honest answer has two halves. The first: most of what is in here costs money everywhere else — a gym, a pantry, a microwave, a quiet room, a place to park yourself between classes — and here it is already paid for. The second: the building went up in 2009 on a bond that runs through 2038, about a third of the operating budget every year. That payment does not stop if attendance drops. It is worth getting something back for it.
